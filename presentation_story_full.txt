================================================================
ENTRY THESIS: IT IS THE FEEDBACK LOOP, NOT THE COMPLEXITY
================================================================

LLMs handle complex but PROCEDURAL tasks well. "Build API +
queue + database + dashboard + tests + deploy" is large, but
each step has a locally verifiable output -- code compiles or
not, test passes or not, endpoint responds or not, container
starts or not. The model acts as a generator of plans, code,
configs, fixes; verification is cheap and the loop closes by
itself.

LLMs struggle with EMPIRICAL-ITERATIVE tasks. "Build a signal-
filtering / MLOps pipeline that separates signal from noise" is
not bigger -- it is structurally different. The quality of
step N depends on interpreting the noisy output of step N-1,
choosing the right metric, and correcting a working hypothesis:

  - did the filter remove signal along with the noise?
  - does the metric measure what we want, or a proxy?
  - did a fix on one slice break another?
  - is the improvement causal, or a benchmark artefact?
  - does the chosen threshold / window / band have a physical
    justification?
  - is the bug in the filter, the data, the labels, the metric,
    the model, or in drift?

Without DSP grounding, an LLM iterates metric-by-metric,
proposes increasingly heavy solutions (CNN-on-spectrograms when
a notch would do), and has no pruning mechanism. Every step
optimises locally; the path is not optimised. Domain knowledge
supplies the missing closed experimental loop: which knob to
turn, what to measure, when to stop, whether a win is real.

Literature anchors:

  SensorBench (sensor-processing benchmark) -- LLMs match
  experts on simple sensor tasks but fail on compositional ones
  and on parameter selection. Self-verification and iterative
  sanity checks improve results. The bottleneck is feedback,
  not text decomposition.

  IMPROVE (agentic ML-pipeline work) -- optimising the whole
  pipeline before evaluation prevents attributing wins to
  specific changes. Iterative single-component refinement
  against real training feedback (the way experienced ML
  engineers work) is more stable.

The four projects below are an existence proof for this thesis.
Signal_generation builds a structured, decomposable noise model.
Signal_detection_RAW and Signal_detection_assist must derive
every filter parameter from the noisy input alone -- the
blindness rule is not a stunt, it is the part of the argument
that proves domain reasoning, not oracle access, drives the
wins. Training_model is the classifier loop that tells us
whether the filter actually helped, and is where the most
counter-intuitive result of the whole story (M_v2 = 0.332,
chance) lives.

FROM CLEAN AUDIO TO A NOISE-PROOF GENRE CLASSIFIER
A four-project story for DSS 2026, told in chronological order
================================================================

The work spans four sibling projects that share a single working
directory tree. Each one solved a piece of the same end-to-end
problem: realistic instrumentation noise, a denoising chain that
reverses it, and a learner that survives both.

  Signal_generation/        builds the data and the noise model
  Signal_detection_RAW/     blind denoising of three unknown signals
  Signal_detection_assist/  staged filter chain on the music dataset
  Training_model/           classifier trained and stressed under noise

The numbers below come from the actual run logs (probe_noise,
NOISE_PATTERN_ANALYSIS, COMPONENT_MATCHED_v1, the train_cross_*
scripts). Every claim about a recovery percentage or a per-genre
accuracy can be re-derived from results/raw_results_*.json under
Training_model/.


PROLOGUE: WHAT A REALISTIC NOISE ENVIRONMENT LOOKS LIKE
--------------------------------------------------------------

The first project, Signal_generation, refused to use white Gaussian
noise as a stand-in for "interference." Real EMC contamination has
structure: it is transient, narrowband, and broadband all at once,
and a good measurement chain adds its own thermal floor, drift and
quantisation on top. The pipeline we built injects seven separately
motivated components into every clean signal:

  EMC layer:
    1. mains hum + harmonics 50 / 100 / 150 / 200 Hz
    2. switching / clock tone at 0.40 * Nyquist (~4410 Hz @ 22050)
    3. broadband EMI -- band-passed white over [0.05, 0.95]*Nyq
    4. impulsive bursts -- 3 per second, exp-decay, tau = 5 ms

  Measurement layer:
    5. thermal white at 0.6 * N(0,1)
    6. 1/f-like drift (cumulative random walk, weighted 1.5)
    7. 8-bit quantisation noise

  Final non-linearity:
    8. ADC saturation -- hard clip at +/- 3 * peak(|clean|)

A single scale alpha forces the sum to a configurable SNR target.
At SNR = -10 dB (current setting) the noise carries ten times the
signal power; the original waveform is conceptually present but
substantially obscured, and three quarters of the music tracks
trigger the clip.

Three demo signals exercise the model end-to-end -- a 10 s ECG
record from MIT-BIH, a 3 s CWRU bearing vibration trace, and a 4 s
piano excerpt -- and the same generator is used by
build_music_dataset.py to contaminate a 250-track music corpus
(jazz 84, metal 83, pop 83, fs = 22050 Hz, ~30 s per track) for
the ML experiments. Per-track seeding (sha256(file_id)) gives every
track its own independent noise realisation while keeping the
relative composition identical -- the same noise statistics, drawn
fresh.

This dataset (clean and noisy halves, paired by file_id) is the
only thing the next three projects share with Signal_generation.
The generator code, the noise spec, and the matched clean WAVs
under Samples/Clean/ are off-limits to the filter and classifier
projects; everything those downstream projects do has to be
derived from the noisy waveforms themselves.


CHAPTER 1 (Signal_detection_RAW): BLIND DENOISING WITH NOTHING BUT A WAVEFORM
--------------------------------------------------------------

The first denoising project was deliberately handed three opaque
WAVs -- ecg_noisy.wav, vibration_noisy.wav, music_noisy.wav -- and
told nothing else. No labels for what the signals were, no spec
for what kinds of noise sat on top. The hard rule: every cutoff,
every notch centre, every Q comes from inspecting the noisy input;
no clean reference is read.

The pipeline: inspect (time + Welch PSD + spectrogram) -> design
a per-signal zero-phase chain -> apply with sosfiltfilt.

What inspection alone delivered:

  ecg (fs = 360 Hz, 10 s)
      observed: large negative DC, signal band collapses into the
                noise floor above ~14 Hz, sharp tonals at 50/72/100 Hz
      chain:    HP Butter ord 4 @ 0.5 Hz . LP Butter ord 6 @ 30 Hz
      result:   var(noisy)/var(filt) = 10.54x

  vibration (fs = 12 kHz, 3 s)
      observed: small DC, 50/100/150 Hz mains family, real signal
                content as resonant clusters at ~1 kHz and ~2.4 kHz,
                pure noise floor above ~2.5 kHz
      chain:    HP @ 5 Hz . three notches at 50/100/150 Hz (Q=30)
                . LP Butter ord 8 @ 2800 Hz
      result:   var reduction 3.54x; 1 kHz / 2.4 kHz resonances
                preserved as the actual measurement.

  music (fs = 22.05 kHz, 4 s)
      observed: rumble below ~20 Hz, 50/100 Hz mains, a tight pair
                of peaks at 4403/4417 Hz (14 Hz apart, ~20 dB above
                local floor) -- too sharp to be musical content
      chain:    HP @ 25 Hz . notches 50/100 Hz (Q=30)
                . notch 4410 Hz (Q=110, BW ~ 40 Hz)
                NO broadband LP -- music carries content to Nyquist
      result:   var reduction 2.96x

The most important decision in this chapter was a refusal: there
was no measurement-based justification for a low-pass on music, so
none was applied. The music PSD outside DC, mains and 4410 Hz is
left untouched. That self-restraint matters, because the next
project would discover that the right band-limit decision is
*signal-specific* -- and the decision changes again once a model
sits downstream of the filter.


CHAPTER 2 (Signal_detection_assist): A STAGED FILTER CHAIN BUILT FROM THE NOISY DATA
--------------------------------------------------------------

The second denoising project worked on the 250-track music dataset
specifically, with the same blindness rule (clean is for evaluation
only; never used to design a filter). Its goal was richer: build a
chain that could feed a downstream classifier, and quantify SNR
recovery and feature-space distance, not just variance reduction.

It opened with a noise pattern analysis (NOISE_PATTERN_ANALYSIS/):
across 24 noisy music files (8 per genre), the bottom-decile RMS
frames were pooled to build a noise-only PSD, and find_peaks was
used to extract the dominant tonal lines. The recovered table:

   freq_Hz   prominence_over_continuum
      51.1   22.0 dB
      99.6   15.4 dB
    4417.0   15.3 dB
    4403.5   15.2 dB
     150.7   14.3 dB
     199.2   10.5 dB

This recovers the 50-Hz mains family and the switching tone at
~4410 Hz from noisy audio alone, without ever opening noise.md.
A drift estimate (~9 % of noise power below 30 Hz) and a partial
burst-rate estimate (envelope > median + 8 MAD) confirmed the
slow-baseline and impulsive components qualitatively.

Filters were then designed as a chain of named "stages", each
addressing a measurement from the analysis:

  Stage 1 -- low-pass / DC + rumble removal
  Stage 2 -- assist + AI denoiser (classical chain followed by an
             AI noise reducer, used as a 2nd-stage refinement)
  Stage 3 -- wavelet / STFT magnitude oracle exploration
  Stage 4 -- STFT-based denoising
  Stage 5 -- spectral subtraction
  Stage 6 -- adaptive mean-subtraction (the AdaptiveMS 6th stage)
  Stage 7 -- ComponentMatched (the production filter)

The Stage 7 chain is the one the classifier project consumes:

  1. Butterworth HP, 25 Hz, order 2, zero-phase
  2. Mains comb -- IIR notches at the *detected* lines
     [51.1, 99.6, 150.7, 199.2] Hz, Q = 35
  3. Switching notch -- centre at the detected 4403.5 Hz peak,
     adaptive Q so width is at least 20 Hz
  4. Adaptive Wiener (Mean-Subtraction + Decision-Directed),
     alpha_p = 0.85, alpha_dd = 0.98, D = 96 frames,
     B_min = 1.3, xi_min = 0.01

The notch frequencies are re-discovered at every run from the
noisy audio. The spec is never consulted. On a 9-track starter set:

  mean noisy  SNR = -6.25 dB   feat-distance = 13.83
  mean stage7 SNR = +2.24 dB   feat-distance =  8.71
                  delta +8.50 dB           delta -5.13

That +8.5 dB SNR gain is the real headline of Chapter 2.

The output WAVs land back under the dataset tree as
  out/Model/Filtered ComponentMatched 7th stage/
so the classifier project can treat Stage 7 as just another
"audio source" in its experiment matrix -- the same way it
treats clean/, noisy/, filtered_AI/, and the earlier stages.


CHAPTER 3 (Training_model): WHAT BREAKS WHEN A MODEL MEETS THE NOISE
--------------------------------------------------------------

The fourth project trained a music genre classifier (jazz / metal
/ pop) on the 250-track corpus and used it as a stress test for
everything upstream. Windows: 3 s, 50 % overlap, track-level
StratifiedGroupKFold so no track ever leaks across folds.

The frozen baseline was a 48-dimensional feature vector -- 20 MFCC
means, 20 MFCC stds, and the mean+std of spectral centroid, rolloff,
bandwidth, and zero-crossing rate -- fed to an RBF-SVM. On the
same folds:

  A  clean -> clean              0.988    upper bound
  B  noisy -> noisy              0.912    noise-aware training
  C  clean -> noisy              0.344    THE CLIFF

99 % to 34 % -- that is what the seven-component noise model does
to a clean-trained model. Closing it became the rest of the story.

3.1  TEST-TIME DENOISERS DON'T CARRY THE LOAD

Plug each denoised version of the dataset into the same clean-trained
classifier:

  D  clean -> filtered_AI         0.404    ( 9.3 % gap recovery)
  E  clean -> filtered_1st        0.340    (-0.6 %)
  F  clean -> filtered_2nd        0.436    (14.3 %)
  G  clean -> adaptivems_6th      0.492    (23.0 %)
  H  clean -> componentmatched_7th 0.560   (33.5 %)

Two patterns mattered. First, the best filter recovered ~34 % of
the gap; B (training on noisy) closed ~88 %. Filtering at test
time is structurally weaker than letting the model see noise during
training. Second -- and more important -- four of the five
clean->filtered conditions collapsed into one dump-target genre
(C dumps to pop, D to jazz, E to metal, G to pop, H to a two-genre
prediction with metal 0.00). Only F produced a real three-way split.
"Highest accuracy" and "no collapse" turned out to be two different
problems.

3.2  MATCH THE MODEL'S TRAINING DOMAIN TO WHAT THE FILTER PRODUCES

The clean-trained classifier was failing on filtered audio because
the train and test distributions disagree where the filter cut
spectral lines. The fix: replicate the linear stages of Stage 7 on
the clean training audio before extracting features -- same HP at
25 Hz, same five notches.

  H_pf   clean_pf -> 7th_pf       0.572   (35.4 % recovery)

The win was real but tiny (+0.012). The notches are ~1.4 Hz wide
relative to MFCC bands of tens to hundreds of Hz, so removing five
thin lines barely shifts any band's energy. The dominant
distribution shift was coming from the data-dependent Wiener stage,
which we cannot replay on clean audio without measuring noise.

The workaround: extend the matched prefilter into a band-pass with
the cutoff just below the switching tone. Below 4 kHz, music
dominates noise; Wiener stays near unity and the filtered audio
is essentially the music with a few thin lines missing. Above
4 kHz the broadband EMI dominates Wiener's behaviour and the
output diverges from clean.

  H_bf4000  HP + notches + LP @ 4000 Hz   0.660   (49.1 % recovery)
  H_bf5500  HP + notches + LP @ 5500 Hz   0.632   (44.7 %)

Per-genre on H_bf4000: jazz 0.92, metal 0.53, pop 0.53 -- the
second cross-condition in the project history (after F) with no
genre near zero, and at much higher overall accuracy. H_bf5500
re-admits the heavily-Wiener-attenuated 4-5.5 kHz band; per-fold
std almost doubles, mean drops, pop slides back toward a dump.
The cutoff has to sit below the switching tone, not above.

Clean-on-clean dropped from 0.988 to ~0.95: the 4 kHz LP throws
away high-frequency timbre that helps with metal on truly-clean
audio. Net: +10 pts on the cross-condition, -4 pts on the upper
bound. Clear win.

3.3  REDESIGN THE FEATURES AROUND THE NOISE WE KNOW IS LEFT

Stage 7 handled mains, switching and drift. What remained?

  - Impulsive bursts (~3/s, ~20 ms): mean+std aggregation is
    sensitive to spikes. Replace with median + MAD (1.4826 * MAD).
    Same dimensionality, burst-resistant.

  - In-band broadband EMI: stationary, so it averages to zero in
    the time-derivative. Add Delta-MFCC and DeltaDelta-MFCC,
    aggregated with the same median + MAD.

  - Clipping: same impulsive signature as bursts; covered for free
    by median + MAD.

None of these required peeking at the noisy files. They responded
to the *types* of noise the dataset contained.

In parallel, the feature set was extended with musically-informed
descriptors so the model could lean on more than timbre when noise
distorted the MFCCs:

  - chroma_stft (12 pitch classes) -- root+5th vs spread chords
    vs major/minor tonics. Below 4 kHz, where the bf4000 prefilter
    keeps content intact.
  - spectral_contrast -- distortion fills valleys (low contrast),
    clean lines stay high contrast.
  - spectral_flatness -- tonal vs noisy.
  - RMS, crest factor, onset rate -- dynamics and rhythm.

The vector grew from 48 dims to 173: 60 MFCC blocks, 8 spectral
scalars, 24 chroma, 14 contrast, 6 flatness/RMS/crest, plus the
onset-rate scalar.

3.4  ALL TOGETHER -- AND ONE COUNTER-RESULT

Same folds, same v2 stack, four pairings:

  A_v2  clean -> clean              0.964 +/- 0.015    sanity
  B_v2  noisy -> noisy              0.916 +/- 0.015    sanity
  H_v2  clean -> 7th                0.796 +/- 0.054    NEW BEST
  M_v2  noisy -> 7th                0.332 +/- 0.010    chance

H_v2 = 0.796 recovers 70 % of the original A-C cliff (vs the
previous best of 49 %). All three genres land above 0.75; the
single-genre collapse is gone.

M_v2 = 0.332 is exactly chance. The noisy-trained model picked
jazz for ~80 % of test tracks. The reason matters: a model trained
on noisy audio doesn't just learn to ignore the noise -- it learns
to use the noise as part of its feature vocabulary. Pop has more
high-frequency content, so it absorbs more broadband EMI energy;
metal peaks loudest and clips most often; jazz has the softest
transients and the lowest burst rate. Those are real, exploitable
correlations *within the noisy distribution*. When Stage 7 strips
them out, the noisy-trained classifier loses the very features it
relied on.

  - B works (noisy->noisy):  noise present in both train and test.
  - H works (clean->7th):    noise absent in train, mostly removed
                             in test. The filter aims for the clean
                             distribution and gets close enough.
  - M fails (noisy->7th):    train and test on opposite sides of
                             the noise -- the worst pairing.

WHAT THE FOUR PROJECTS, TOGETHER, TAUGHT US
--------------------------------------------------------------

1. NOISE HAS STRUCTURE. Modelling it as broadband Gaussian misses
   most of what hurts a real measurement -- mains lines, switching
   tones, impulsive bursts and slow drift dominate the post-mix.
   Simulating each component separately is what makes every
   downstream experiment interpretable: when something fails, we
   can point at which component it failed against.

2. BLIND FILTER DESIGN WORKS WHEN MEASUREMENTS DRIVE EVERY CHOICE.
   Both Signal_detection_RAW and Signal_detection_assist
   reconstructed the noise spec from the noisy waveforms alone,
   without ever opening noise.md. Variance reduction up to 10x on
   single-domain signals; +8.5 dB SNR on the music corpus from a
   four-stage chain whose notch frequencies were re-discovered
   at every run.

3. RESTRAINT IS A FILTER STAGE. In Chapter 1 we refused a music
   LP because nothing in the spectrum justified one. In Chapter 3
   we *added* an LP at exactly 4 kHz because the model downstream
   needed the train and test feature support to coincide on the
   filter's high-confidence band. Same band-limit knob; opposite
   decisions. Both correct, because the success criterion was
   different.

4. WHEN THE TEST PIPELINE INCLUDES A DENOISER, TRAIN AND TEST
   FEATURE DISTRIBUTIONS HAVE TO END UP ON THE SAME SIDE OF THE
   NOISE. Stage 7 recovers (an approximation of) the clean
   distribution, so train on clean. Train on noisy only when the
   test audio is also noisy. Crossing the noise boundary is the
   worst pairing -- M_v2 made this concrete at 0.332.

5. ROBUSTNESS COMES FROM FEATURE DESIGN, NOT JUST HYPERPARAMETER
   TUNING. Replacing mean+std with median+MAD costs nothing and
   handles bursts and clipping. Delta and DeltaDelta MFCCs
   neutralise stationary in-band noise. Chroma and spectral
   contrast add a second axis of class discrimination that timbre
   alone misses. The full v2 stack was the first thing that broke
   the single-genre collapse outright.

6. DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE OUTPERFORMS BRUTE TUNING ON A SMALL DATASET.
   Each feature-design step here can be traced back to a single
   sentence in the noise spec or a single musical fact about the
   genres -- not to a grid search.

7. THE SINGLE-GENRE COLLAPSE PATTERN IS A SIGNAL ABOUT THE MODEL,
   NOT THE GENRES. Every collapse we saw -- pop dump, jazz dump,
   metal disappearance -- meant the classifier had latched onto a
   feature that broke under the test-time perturbation. Each fix
   that changed the dump target without breaking it was a partial
   solution. The full v2 stack on Stage-7 audio (H_v2) was the
   first end-to-end pairing that produced a balanced three-way
   prediction at competitive accuracy.
