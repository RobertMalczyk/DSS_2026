# Noise model — exact specification and physical origin

This document describes every disturbance component injected by
`signal_contamination.py` into the three test signals (ECG, vibration, audio),
and — using the same model — by `build_music_dataset.py` into the 250-track
GTZAN music dataset under `out/Model/`.

The noise model is identical in both contexts and the SNR target is currently
the same too:

| Context | Module | SNR target | Files produced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Three demo signals | `signal_contamination.py` | **−10 dB** | `out/*_plots.png`, `out/summary.txt` |
| Music dataset | `build_music_dataset.py` | **−10 dB** | `out/Model/noisy/*.wav` + `labels.csv` |

Sections 1–5 below describe the components and numbers at the −10 dB
reference. Section 6 explains the small differences that remain between the
two contexts (per-track seeding and clipping statistics) and how to re-tune
the music dataset if a different SNR is needed.

All numbers assume RNG seed 42 globally, plus the per-track noise seed
`sha256(file_id)` for the music dataset so each track gets its own realisation.

---

## 1. Global parameters (shared across all three signals)

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| RNG seed | 42 (NumPy `default_rng`) |
| Target total SNR | −10 dB |
| Mains fundamental | 50 Hz |
| Switching-tone frequency | 0.40 × Nyquist |
| Impulsive burst rate | 3 / s |
| Burst decay time constant | 5 ms (exponential) |
| ADC resolution | 8 bits |
| ADC saturation clip | ± 3 × peak(|clean|), applied after summation |

## 2. Composite formula

```
emc          = hum + switching + broadband + bursts
measurement  = 0.6·white + 1.5·drift + 1.0·quant
α            = sqrt( P_signal / 10^(SNR/10) / mean((emc+measurement)²) )
total_noise  = α · (emc + measurement)
noisy        = clip( clean + total_noise , ±3·peak(|clean|) )
```

The single scaling constant α is what forces every signal to the same −10 dB.
Measured values for this run:

| Signal | α |
|---|---|
| ECG | 0.4061 |
| Vibration | 0.08509 |
| Audio | 0.1089 |

Because α multiplies both `emc` and `measurement`, the *relative* composition
of every component is identical across the three signals — only absolute
amplitude differs.

## 3. EMC components

### 3.1 Mains hum + harmonics *(narrowband EMC)*

**Physical origin.** 50 Hz AC mains coupled into the measurement chain through
capacitive pickup on unshielded leads, inductive pickup from power
transformers, and ground loops between instruments sharing an earth.
Harmonics (100, 150, 200 Hz) are generated by non-linear loads — switched-mode
supplies, rectifiers, fluorescent ballasts, variable-frequency drives — that
clip the mains waveform and inject third- and higher-order components.

**Math.** Sum of sines with random phases:

    hum(t) = Σ_k A_k · sin(2π · k · 50 · t + φ_k),    φ_k ~ U(0, 2π)

with A_k = {1.0, 0.5, 0.3, 0.15} at k = 1…4, truncated when
`k · 50 ≥ 0.95 · Nyquist`.

| Signal | Harmonics present | Raw RMS | Post-α RMS |
|---|---|---|---|
| ECG (Nyq 180 Hz) | 50, 100, 150 Hz | 0.819 | **0.332 mV** |
| Vibration (Nyq 6 kHz) | 50, 100, 150, 200 Hz | 0.825 | **0.0702 g** |
| Audio (Nyq 24 kHz) | 50, 100, 150, 200 Hz | 0.825 | **0.0899** |

### 3.2 Switching / clock tone *(narrowband EMC)*

**Physical origin.** Any periodic on/off power stage nearby: DC/DC buck or
boost converter, USB-PD adapter, class-D audio amplifier carrier, digital
clock / PLL reference, fluorescent-lamp ballast, induction-motor inverter.
Real switching frequencies sit between ~20 kHz and a few MHz; when aliased or
coupled through a lower-bandwidth analog front-end they appear as a narrow
spectral line plus slow drift, which is emulated here with a small FM.

**Math.**

    f_sw  = 0.40 · Nyquist
    Δf    = 0.002 · f_sw                          (≈0.2 % FM deviation)
    φ(t)  = 2π · f_sw · t + 2π · ∫₀ᵗ Δf · sin(2π · 0.7 · τ) dτ
    sw(t) = 0.6 · sin(φ(t))

| Signal | f_sw | FM deviation | Post-α RMS |
|---|---|---|---|
| ECG | 72 Hz | ±0.144 Hz | **0.172 mV** |
| Vibration | 2 400 Hz | ±4.8 Hz | **0.0361 g** |
| Audio | 9 600 Hz | ±19.2 Hz | **0.0462** |

### 3.3 Broadband EMI *(wideband EMC)*

**Physical origin.** Aggregate radiated and conducted interference from fast
digital electronics, RF links (cellular / Wi-Fi / Bluetooth / ISM-band radios),
spark-commutated motors, brush arcing, and VFD-driven machinery. Averaged
over a long window it looks approximately Gaussian and roughly flat across
the instrument's passband.

**Math.** Unit-variance white Gaussian passed through a zero-phase 4th-order
Butterworth bandpass [0.05·Nyq, 0.95·Nyq], scaled by 0.8.

| Signal | Passband | Post-α RMS |
|---|---|---|
| ECG | 9 – 171 Hz | **0.307 mV** |
| Vibration | 300 – 5 700 Hz | **0.0643 g** |
| Audio | 1 200 – 22 800 Hz | **0.0823** |

### 3.4 Impulsive bursts *(transient EMC)*

**Physical origin.** ESD events (finger or tool contact), relay / contactor
switching, inductive-load turn-off spikes (solenoids, motor windings), arc
welding, lightning-surge remnants, brush sparking on commutator motors, engine
ignition noise. Each event is short, high-amplitude, spectrally broad, and
arrives at irregular times.

**Math.** `N_B = round(3 · duration)` bursts at uniformly random indices.
Each burst is Gaussian noise multiplied by an exponential decay with
τ = 5 ms and a random ±amplitude drawn from U(2.5, 5.0):

    burst[i : i+L] += A · sign · exp(-n/τ_samples) · N(0,1)_n,   n = 0…L-1

| Signal | # bursts | Burst length | Post-α RMS |
|---|---|---|---|
| ECG | 30 | 8 samples (22 ms floor) | **0.206 mV** |
| Vibration | 9 | 240 samples (20 ms) | **0.0316 g** |
| Audio | 12 | 960 samples (20 ms) | **0.0370** |

Peak instantaneous amplitude after α exceeds the ±3·peak(|clean|) ADC clip on
all three signals, which is why every run reports `clipped = YES`.

## 4. Measurement-chain components

### 4.1 Thermal / Johnson–Nyquist noise

**Physical origin.** Irreducible thermal agitation of charge carriers in every
resistive element of the analog front-end — `v_n² = 4·k_B·T·R·Δf` — plus the
op-amp's input-referred voltage-noise density. Flat (white) across the usable
bandwidth.

**Math.** `0.6 · N(0,1)`, drawn per sample.

Post-α RMS: **0.245 mV (ECG) · 0.0512 g (vibration) · 0.0652 (audio)**.

### 4.2 1/f-like baseline drift

**Physical origin.** Flicker noise in semiconductor junctions and MOSFETs
(dominant below ~1 kHz), slow thermal drift of amplifier offsets, electrode
half-cell polarisation and skin-electrode impedance drift for ECG,
motion-induced DC offsets, reference-voltage drift.

**Math.** Cumulative sum of unit-variance Gaussian samples (discrete-time
random walk), normalised to unit standard deviation, weighted 1.5. The
cumulative sum has PSD ∝ 1/f², which is stronger than a true 1/f law and
intentionally dominates low-frequency power.

Post-α RMS: **0.609 mV (ECG) · 0.128 g (vibration) · 0.163 (audio)** — the
single largest component in every signal, responsible for the slow baseline
meander visible in the plots.

### 4.3 Quantization noise

**Physical origin.** Finite ADC resolution plus any downstream fixed-point
truncation. An 8-bit converter spanning the clean signal's full swing produces
an LSB of `q = 2 · peak(|clean|) / 256`. Quantisation error is typically
modelled as uniform on `[−q/2, q/2]` when the input is sufficiently busy — we
*add* that uniform noise rather than actually re-quantising.

| Signal | LSB `q` | Theoretical σ_q = q/√12 | Post-α RMS |
|---|---|---|---|
| ECG | 7.50 × 10⁻³ mV | 2.16 × 10⁻³ mV | **8.7 × 10⁻⁴ mV** |
| Vibration | 2.24 × 10⁻³ g | 6.46 × 10⁻⁴ g | **5.5 × 10⁻⁵ g** |
| Audio | 4.68 × 10⁻³ | 1.35 × 10⁻³ | **1.5 × 10⁻⁴** |

Intentionally the smallest contributor — a real 16/24-bit ADC would make this
negligible, and even the 8-bit model used here still sits well below the EMC
and drift contributions.

### 4.4 ADC saturation *(final non-linearity)*

**Physical origin.** A real converter has a finite input range; when signal +
drift + burst exceeds that range the sample is clamped instead of wrapping.
Implemented as a hard symmetric clip at `±3 · peak(|clean|)` applied to
`clean + total_noise`. All three signals trigger it in the current run.

| Signal | RMS clean | RMS total noise | Peak clean | Peak noise (pre-clip) | Dominant contributor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ECG | 0.362 mV | 1.146 mV | ~1.6 mV | 5.3 mV | 1/f drift, then broadband EMI and hum |

## 6. Music dataset (250 GTZAN tracks)

The ML dataset under `out/Model/` reuses the exact same seven-component noise
model at the same −10 dB SNR target as the demo signals. Two things still
differ from the demo:

### 6.1 Per-track noise seeds

The demo signals all use the single global `SEED = 42`. Each music track
instead gets an independent noise realisation from

    seed_k = int.from_bytes( sha256(file_id).digest()[:4], "big" )

so `jazz_01.wav`, `jazz_02.wav`, … are contaminated by statistically identical
but independently drawn noise. This matters for ML training: the model sees
250 independent draws from the same distribution rather than one noise track
repeated 250 times.

### 6.2 Clipping rate at −10 dB

The ADC saturation clip (`±3 · peak(|clean|)`, applied after summation)
triggers on roughly three-quarters of the 250 music tracks. The un-clipped
subset is dominated by quieter pieces (mostly jazz) whose own peak leaves
more headroom for the noise excursions before saturation. The noisy music
recordings therefore contain occasional flat-topped regions around impulsive
bursts — a feature any downstream model will encounter.

### 6.3 Re-tuning the music-dataset noise level

Change a single constant in `build_music_dataset.py` and rerun it:

```python
NOISY_SNR_DB = -10.0      # current (baseline, ~3/4 of tracks clipped)
# NOISY_SNR_DB = -16.02   # 2x amplitude, ~98 % of tracks clipped
# NOISY_SNR_DB =  -3.0    # light contamination, signal clearly visible
```

Track picks (`rng.choice` with `GLOBAL_SEED = 42`) and per-track noise seeds
(SHA-256 of `file_id`) stay deterministic, so only the scale factor α shifts
and every component's RMS moves with it.

## 7. Reproducing these numbers

```
PY="/c/Users/robak/AppData/Local/Programs/Python/Python312/python.exe"
"$PY" signal_contamination.py     # regenerates demo plots at -10 dB
"$PY" probe_noise.py              # dumps per-component raw RMS + α (-10 dB)
"$PY" build_music_dataset.py      # regenerates 250 clean+noisy music tracks (-16.02 dB)
```

`probe_noise.py` prints the raw (pre-α) RMS of each component and the scale
factor α for every signal; multiplying raw-RMS × α reproduces the Post-α
numbers in Sections 3–4, and multiplying by an **extra factor of 2** gives
the music-dataset values.
